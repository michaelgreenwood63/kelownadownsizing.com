Downsizing Checklist: What to Keep, Sell, or Donate

July 2026 · By Mark Jontz & Associates · 8 min read

The real estate part of downsizing is usually the easier half. The harder part, for most people, is deciding what happens to 20-30 years of belongings — and doing it without the whole process turning into an overwhelming, emotionally exhausting slog. The single biggest factor in how that goes isn't willpower, it's timing: start months before your move, not the week before.

Start with a simple four-category system

For every item, every room, ask which of these four it belongs to:

  • Keep — comes with you to the new place.
  • Sell — has real resale value (furniture, tools, collectibles, vehicles).
  • Donate — usable but not worth the effort of selling.
  • Family heirlooms to pass on now — items with sentimental or family value that you'd rather hand down while you can enjoy watching them be appreciated, rather than leaving that decision to someone else later.

That fourth category is easy to skip but genuinely valuable — many people find giving a specific item to a specific person now, with the story behind it, far more meaningful than it becoming "stuff to deal with" in an estate later.

Measure your new space before you decide anything

Before you agonize over any single piece of furniture, get the actual room dimensions of your target home (or a realistic range if you haven't chosen yet) and measure your current furniture against them. A huge percentage of "should I keep this?" decisions resolve themselves the moment you realize something simply won't physically fit.

Work room by room, not all at once

Trying to decide about the whole house at once is how this process becomes overwhelming. Pick one room, finish it completely, then move to the next. Kitchens and garages tend to take the longest — start there first if you're working with a deadline, so the easier rooms are left for when your energy is lower.

Set a real deadline, then work backward

If you're planning to list your home in a set number of months, work backward from that date and assign each room a target completion week. Without a deadline, sorting belongings has a way of expanding to fill all available time.

Know your local options before you start

Knowing where things actually go removes a lot of decision fatigue:

  • Consignment and estate sale companies — worth it for furniture and higher-value items; they typically take a percentage but handle the pricing and selling for you.
  • Local donation centres — most accept furniture and household goods in good condition; call ahead, as pickup availability and accepted items vary.
  • Online marketplaces — best for smaller, higher-demand items you're willing to coordinate pickup for yourself.
  • Family and friends — often the fastest path for furniture and heirlooms, and the most meaningful one.

The honest truth about this process: it's rarely about logic — most of the difficulty is emotional, not practical. Give yourself permission to move slower through the genuinely hard decisions (a parent's belongings, items tied to a specific memory) while moving faster through the easy ones (duplicate kitchen tools, clothes you haven't worn in years). Treating every item with equal weight is what makes this feel impossible.

When to start

If you're even considering downsizing in the next year, start sorting now — not when you list, and not after you've accepted an offer. The homeowners who find this process manageable are almost always the ones who gave themselves months, not weeks.
