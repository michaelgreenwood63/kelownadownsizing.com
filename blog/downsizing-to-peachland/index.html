Downsizing to Peachland: What Kelowna-Area Sellers Should Know

October 2026 · By Mark Jontz & Associates · 6 min read

Of the communities we cover — Kelowna, West Kelowna, Lake Country, and Peachland — Peachland is the one most specifically built around an older population, and it's worth understanding why before you rule it in or out as a downsizing destination.

Peachland's Age Profile Is Unusual, Even for the Okanagan

Over 50% of Peachland's population is over the age of 55. That's not a marketing line — it's the figure the Province itself cited when announcing new seniors housing there. The practical effect is a small town that's genuinely oriented around an older demographic, rather than one where retirees are a minority tucked into a younger community.

A Real, Active Seniors Housing Pipeline

Peachland isn't just demographically older — it's actively building for it. The Residences on 5th, a 73-unit affordable seniors rental building, opened in spring 2026, joining the neighbouring Residences on 6th (completed 2019). Together, the two buildings provide 147 homes specifically designed to let seniors age in place within the community — accessible units, a mix of one- and two-bedroom layouts, and purpose-built accessibility and comfort features.

That matters beyond the rental buildings themselves: a community actively investing in this kind of infrastructure tends to have the surrounding supports — medical access, transit consideration, walkable amenities — that make downsizing somewhere actually livable long-term, not just affordable on paper.

What the Market Actually Looks Like

Peachland's housing stock is smaller and more varied than Kelowna's — a mix of lakefront and lake-view properties, smaller-town single-family homes, and a growing number of condo and townhome options aimed specifically at downsizers. Pricing varies significantly by property type and view, more than in a larger, more homogenous market — which makes a general "average price" number less useful here than getting real comparables pulled for the specific type of home you're considering.

Worth knowing: Peachland is a genuinely small, close-knit community — population under 6,000. That's part of its appeal for a lot of downsizers, but it also means fewer active listings at any given time than Kelowna proper. If a specific type of property interests you there, timing and being ready to move matter more than in a larger market with constant inventory turnover.

How This Fits Your Broader Decision

Peachland is one option among several in the Okanagan, and it won't be the right fit for everyone — proximity to family, medical specialists, and amenities in Kelowna proper are real tradeoffs against the smaller-town pace and lake-focused lifestyle Peachland offers. If you're weighing it against staying in Kelowna or looking at West Kelowna or Lake Country instead, that's exactly the kind of area-by-area comparison worth having before you commit to a search in any one of them.
