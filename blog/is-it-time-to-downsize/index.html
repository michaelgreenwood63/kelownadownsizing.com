Is It Time to Downsize? 7 Signs to Watch For

July 2026 · By Mark Jontz & Associates · 6 min read

There's no birthday that automatically means it's time to downsize. We've worked with homeowners in their late 50s who were ready, and homeowners in their 80s who weren't — yet. Age is a poor predictor on its own. What actually predicts a successful downsize is a handful of recognizable, practical signals. Here are the seven we hear most often from Okanagan homeowners who ended up glad they made the move.

1. You're paying to maintain space you don't use

Walk through your home and count the rooms that exist mainly for guests who visit once or twice a year. Every one of those rooms still needs heating, cleaning, insuring, and eventually repairing — you're paying an ongoing cost for occasional use.

2. Stairs have gone from convenient to a genuine concern

A two-storey layout that felt completely natural at 45 can quietly become a daily obstacle course decades later. If you're already avoiding the upstairs, or thinking about how you'd manage the basement stairs if your knees got worse, that's worth taking seriously rather than working around indefinitely.

3. Maintenance feels like a chore, not a hobby

Some people genuinely enjoy yard work and home projects well into their later years — if that's you, this sign doesn't apply. But if mowing, gutters, and the ever-present list of "should really get to that" repairs have shifted from satisfying to draining, a lower-maintenance property changes your day-to-day relationship with where you live.

4. You're sitting on equity you're not using

Many long-time Okanagan homeowners have significant equity built up in a home that's grown in value for 15-20+ years. That equity is doing nothing for you while it's tied up in square footage you don't need. Downsizing can release real capital for retirement income, travel, helping family, or simply more financial breathing room.

5. You've started thinking about "what if something happens"

If you've caught yourself wondering how you'd manage the house alone if a spouse's health changed, or how you'd handle a bad winter without help clearing snow, that's a meaningful signal. It's far better to make this move on your own timeline, while you have full choice in the matter, than to be forced into a rushed decision during a crisis.

6. You want a built-in community

A family-oriented neighbourhood doesn't always offer much social connection for someone in a different life stage. 55+ communities specifically are designed around this — quieter environments, neighbours going through similar transitions, and often organized social activities that a standard neighbourhood simply doesn't have.

7. The thought of downsizing brings relief, not just anxiety

This one is less concrete but genuinely important: pay attention to your actual emotional reaction when you picture a smaller home. If it's mostly anxiety about loss, you may not be ready yet, and that's fine — there's no deadline. If underneath the logistics stress there's a real feeling of relief at the idea of less to manage, that's usually a sign the timing is closer than you think.

The honest bottom line: if you're nodding along to three or more of these, it's worth at least having a real conversation — not necessarily listing your home tomorrow, but understanding what your options actually look like.
