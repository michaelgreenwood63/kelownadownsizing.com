Is Late Summer a Good Time to List the Home You're Downsizing From?

August 2026 · By Mark Jontz & Associates · 4 min read

If you're planning a downsize, the calendar probably matters less than your own readiness — but late summer does come with a few genuine, practical advantages worth knowing about as you think through timing.

Fewer Competing Family-Home Listings

Families with school-age kids tend to finish their moves before the school year starts, which means fewer larger family homes competing for buyer attention in late August and September. If you're selling a larger home to downsize, that's slightly less noise around your listing.

Serious Buyers Are Back from Summer

Just like the broader Kelowna market, buyers interested in the kind of home you're likely selling — established, move-in ready — tend to re-engage after summer travel wraps up, giving you a more active buyer pool than the quieter mid-summer weeks.

What Matters More Than the Season

For most downsizers, the real driver of timing isn't the market calendar — it's whether you've sorted out what you're keeping, where you're moving to, and whether you need to sell before buying or can manage both at once. Rushing a listing to hit a "good month" when you're not actually ready to move rarely pays off.

Our honest advice: get your own logistics sorted first — what you're keeping, where you're going, your sell-first-or-buy-first plan. Once those are settled, late summer is a reasonably good window to list, but it's not going to fix a plan that isn't ready yet.
