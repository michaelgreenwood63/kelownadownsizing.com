Section 02
Is It Time
To Downsize?

The decision to downsize is rarely made overnight. For most people, it's a gradual realization — a growing sense that the home no longer fits the life they want to live. If any of the situations below sound familiar, it may be worth having a conversation.

💰
Financial Considerations
Your housing costs — mortgage, taxes, utilities, maintenance — have risen above 30% of your income. A smaller property could meaningfully reduce your monthly expenses and free up capital for retirement, travel, or family.
🏠
Too Much Unused Space
Rooms sit empty. Closets are full of things you haven't touched in years. You're maintaining space you no longer use — and paying for it every month.
👨‍👩‍👧
Empty Nest
The kids have moved out and the home feels bigger than it needs to be. Many downsizers describe this phase as a natural turning point — an opportunity to reset for the next chapter.
🔧
Maintenance Overload
Keeping up with the yard, the repairs, the cleaning — it's become a part-time job. Downsizing to a smaller home or strata property can dramatically reduce the time and money spent on upkeep.
❤️
Health & Accessibility
Stairs are becoming more difficult. You'd prefer to be closer to medical services, family, or transit. Planning ahead means you choose on your terms — not in a rush.
✈️
Lifestyle Change
You want to travel more, spend less time maintaining a property, or move to a community with more social activity. A smaller home supports a bigger life.
If you've identified with even one of these, it may be time for a conversation. There's no obligation — just honest advice.

Section 05
Step 1 —
Decluttering

For most people, decluttering is the most emotionally and physically demanding part of downsizing — and the most important to start early. The key is to approach it systematically rather than all at once.

Start with larger items first. Work room by room, beginning with furniture, appliances, and bulky items. Then move to smaller spaces — closets, storage rooms, crawlspaces, and junk drawers. As spaces open up, the process becomes progressively more manageable.

Tip from our team
Commit to one room or one category per weekend. Trying to do everything at once leads to burnout. Small, consistent progress adds up faster than you'd expect.

The Four-Pile Sorting System is the most effective method we've seen across hundreds of downsizing moves. Every item gets placed into one of four categories:

Keep It
You use it regularly and it fits in your new space
Sell It
Still has value — Facebook Marketplace, estate sale, or consignment
Donate It
Good condition but you don't need it — give it a new home
Toss It
No longer useful or in poor condition — let it go

For sentimental items, involve your family early. Many clients find that offering meaningful pieces to children or grandchildren is one of the most rewarding parts of the process — and it ensures those items are genuinely appreciated.

Step 2 — Budget
& Finances

Before you list your home or start shopping for your next property, it's essential to understand your complete financial picture. Downsizing should improve your financial situation — but only if you go in with clear numbers.

Start with your equity. Your home's current market value minus your mortgage balance is your starting point. From there, subtract the costs of selling (see Section 11 for a full breakdown) and you'll have a clear picture of what you'll net.

Key question to answer early
What do you want your monthly housing costs to look like after you downsize? Work backwards from that number. It'll help define your price range for your next home and clarify whether downsizing achieves your financial goals.

Build your full budget. Include every cost associated with the move — not just the sale and purchase prices. Common items people underestimate:

Should I Buy
or Sell First?

This is the question we hear most often — and there's no single right answer. It depends on your financial situation, your timeline, and your comfort with risk. Here are the three options most of our clients consider:

Option 01
Sell
First
Sell your current home first and rent until you purchase your next property. You'll know exactly how much you have to spend and won't be carrying two mortgages.
✓ Know your exact budget
✓ No double mortgage risk
✗ May need to rent temporarily
✗ Two moves instead of one
Option 02
Buy
First
Find and purchase your new home before listing your current one. You move once and avoid temporary housing — but you carry two properties until the first sells.
✓ Move once, no rental
✓ Take time to find the right home
✗ Carrying two mortgages
✗ Pressure to sell quickly
Option 03 — Recommended
Line
Both Up
Use the Home Safe Program to coordinate both transactions simultaneously. The right clauses in your contracts protect you from being caught between two properties.
✓ Move once, seamlessly
✓ Protected by contract conditions
✓ Most common successful approach

Section 10
The Home Safe
Program
How we coordinate the sale of your home with the purchase of your next one — so you move once, seamlessly, with no gaps and no financial risk.
No Double Mortgage
No Temporary Housing
No Obligation to Sell
1
Step One
List Your Home — With Your Protection Clause
We determine your home's market value and put it on the market. Once we receive an offer, we negotiate to include a key contract condition: "Subject to Finding Another Suitable Property." This means the sale of your home is only completed if you successfully find your next property within a set timeframe — typically 30, 60, or 90 days. If you don't find the right home, you are under no obligation to sell.
2
Step Two
Lock In Your Numbers
With a conditional offer accepted on your current home, you now know exactly what price you're selling for and when closing occurs. That certainty is everything — it sets your buying budget precisely, so you can search for your next home knowing exactly what you can spend. No guessing based on an estimated value.
3
Step Three
Make Your Move — Once, Seamlessly
Now you make an offer on your next home, with a condition that your current home's sale completes first. Both transactions run in parallel. On closing day, you hand over the keys to your old home and receive the keys to your new one. One move. No rental. No overlap. Hundreds of our clients have done exactly this.
